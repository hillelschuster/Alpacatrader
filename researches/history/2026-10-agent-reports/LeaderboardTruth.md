# Strategy-family leaderboard — common-basis audit

**Verdict.** Adaptive-depth is the strongest *real-quote, marketable, L1-capacity-gated* candidate measured on the full 2023/late blocks, but it is not validated and its result is conditional on known quote-touch outcomes. It is not the strongest measured headline: retained `sparse_daily` repeat-h60 has larger positive known-fill results on both blocks even after correcting its session denominator to calendar days, and it has a real 1–4 minute proxy-delay plateau. However, it remains a minute-open proxy with no size/volume gate; the execution audit supports at most a sensitivity haircut, not an executable expected return. Under a conservative “only L1-supported names contribute; unsupported attempts earn zero” scenario, sparse-daily's adjusted rate is about $2.27/$3.54 per calendar day (val/late) versus depth's observed known-touch $0.94/$4.11 at 25 bps. Neither passes a whole-book unknown-loss test or pristine OOS validation. Thus: **depth is the best realistic executable *candidate* currently evidenced, sparse-daily is the higher-return but materially less realistic *research lead*.** Neither is a paper-ready profitability claim.

## Basis and interpretation

- Validation is calendar year 2023 (250 sessions spanning 361 calendar days); “late” is 2025-02-03–2026-05-29 (332 sessions, 481 calendar days), previously explored and **not pristine**. No sealed 2024, 2025-01, or 2026-06..08 raw data was opened. “Late” is an out-of-sample-style comparison only.
- Every table dollar rate is **known-fill contribution per full calendar-day span**, not mean per trading session. For session-based proxy artifacts I converted `$/session × sessions ÷ calendar days`; no-fill cash remains zero. Proxy-lane unknowns are stated separately. Annual return is a simple `calendar-day $ × 365 ÷ reserve`, *not CAGR*. Because cost conventions differ, “25/50 bps” is **not a cross-family apples-to-apples fee**: minute-proxy rows charge total modeled friction; quote-touch rows charge residual friction on top of real ASK/BID prices, which already embed spread.
- Reserve is each row's native book. `% reserve/year` cannot be compared as if order size, capacity, or book assumptions were identical. Quote rows have capacity/price support but do not prove exchange fills. Known-fill rates exclude UNKNOWN contributions; none should be represented as whole-portfolio EV.
- `Unknown` = quote/exit outcome not adequately resolved, not cash. A worst-case full-loss floor is a coding bound, not a realistic EV. Ranking is evidence-weighted (execution realism first, then validation and cost plateau), not sorted by late headline dollars.

### Sparse proxy realism haircut: what is and is not supportable

The retained h60 execution audit found **5.2% of sampled entries** and **3.6% of exits** had less whole-minute volume than a $1,000 ticket requires. These are observed inadequate-volume fractions, not a return haircut; adding them (8.8%) assumes independence and is not warranted. More directly, the separate as-of quote/L1 audit on the same retained-h60 family found **79/143 = 55.2%** late intents L1-supported at $1,000 and 250ms / 2s-age rules (VAL 41/61 = 67.2%). As an explicitly crude zero-contribution-for-unsupported sensitivity, applying those observed support shares to repeat-h60's calendar-day proxy rate at 25bps yields roughly **$2.27 VAL / $3.54 LATE** (about a 33%/45% haircut), versus its proxy $3.38/$6.41. This is *not* a fill-weighted estimate: L1 support is endogenous, excludes/doesn't resolve the other 44.8%, and queue/market impact remain. If unresolved capacity/outcomes instead incur the lane's conservative full-ticket-loss coding, results can be much worse; no honest single-point executable return can be derived from the audit. Actual quote-touched returns are the better benchmark.

## Why adaptive depth is #1 here (but not declared “the winner”)

1. It is the only high-ranked family that combines both full-block measurements with observed side-aware ASK/BID touch prices and an explicit causal L1 participation ceiling. That is the decisive credibility edge over the more profitable sparse proxy.
2. Its calendar-normalized 25/50 known-touch contribution is positive VAL and LATE, while proxy timing/exit refinements often reverse across blocks. The 50bps rung remains positive in both blocks; validation falls below zero by 125bps, so do not oversell its friction margin.
3. Its limits are serious: eight validation and twenty late UNKNOWN of 62/142 attempts; worst-case unknown-unit coding drives the whole-book bound negative; bootstrap uncertainty includes zero; 44.3% late win rate, median negative fill, top-5 share >100%; 2025/26 late is not pristine. The independent audit found no mechanical-sign bug, but verified these realism/concentration risks.
4. Sparse repeat-h60's reported $5.68/day headline at 100bps is *per session*, not calendar day. Correct calendar figure is about $3.92/day at 100bps; 25/50 calendar figures are about $6.41/$5.58. It remains a valuable target for a fully paired, quote-priced, capacity-gated test, but its headline cannot outrank observed quote touches without assuming proxy-to-fill equivalence.
5. Entry delay's attractive proxy plateau also attenuates under quote pricing: the d2 actual-touch form is +$1.57 validation / +$2.29 late at 25, and its own late d0 twin is +$3.38. No family is validated, all late evidence is previously explored, and no candidate has the demanded pristine collision months.
